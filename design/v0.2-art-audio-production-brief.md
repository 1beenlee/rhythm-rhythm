# Rhythm Rhythm v0.2 — Rhythm Heaven–caliber Art, Gameplay Presentation & Music Rebuild

Status: **APPROVED CREATIVE DIRECTION / IMPLEMENTATION NEEDED**, not a completed design or build. This is the canonical production brief for the v0.2 branch. The v0.1 technical MVP was *not* accepted as consumer-quality art/audio.

## 0. Mission and quality bar

The owner viewed v0.1 screenshots and rejected their visual and audio quality as QA/demo placeholders. The next output must be an **attractive, playable, polished rhythm-game collection** reminiscent of the *experience/design language* of Nintendo's Rhythm Tengoku (GBA, 2006), Rhythm Heaven (DS), Rhythm Heaven Fever (Wii), and Rhythm Heaven Groove (Switch, 2026). **Do not perform an incremental reskin of the big-button app.** Rebuild the presentation of the home screen, game selection, instruction, three stages, and results around authored art, animation, musical scenes, humor, anticipation, and punchy tactile feedback.

Do not copy Nintendo game files, character likenesses, exact copyrighted songs/melodies, UI images, voice samples, names, logos, or level designs. External reference images are for studying the *composition, timing, simplicity, energy, and art direction* only. All exported assets must be original, openly licensed with checked compatibility, or legitimately generated for this project. Record asset provenance.

The benchmark is “Would someone believe these screenshots and 20-second gameplay videos belong to a delightful finished indie rhythm title, rather than a developer UI?” If not, the gate fails even if tests are green.

Eight owner-supplied snapshots show: a nearly empty blue-white screen with three large utility-style pill buttons; a text-only game menu; a tiny horizontal gray playfield crushed between prominent SCORE/COMBO/BEAT labels and enormous Pause/Restart/Exit buttons; a Beat Factory scene consisting of rectangles and disks; a giant single-color circle with eyes for Echo Planet; and two colored rings/descending dots for Kitchen. “GOOD” overlaps the art title; “GO/READY” overlays action in old-build captures; results are a large numeral on an empty page. Fix the structural problem before ornaments: the game action and expressive characters should dominate the viewport, not the surrounding controls or diagnostics. An older build may account for some overlay/text defects; do not mistake that for resolving the aesthetic critique.

## 2. Reference grammar — study and transform, don't trace

Original reference material:
- Rhythm Tengoku GBA review/screenshots: https://nintendoworldreport.com/review/12018/rhythm-tengoku-game-boy-advance
- Rhythm Heaven Fever review/screenshots: https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/wii/rhythm_heaven_fever
- Nintendo's Iwata Asks developer explanation of sharply legible *instantaneous* sound response: https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/ds/rhythm-heaven/0/2/
- Wii game creative music/visual contrast: https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/rhythmheavenfever/0/1/
- Nintendo's current official Rhythm Heaven Groove gameplay/screens: https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/rhythm-heaven-groove-switch/

Key principles to emulate through original content:
1. One strange, memorable **miniature world per game**, treated like a little cartoon performance.
2. Simple shapes are **drawn and animated with personality**: expressive faces, strong outlines, squash/stretch, visual jokes, motion arcs, credible scene geography—not abstract placeholder rectangles.
3. **One or two simple inputs**, but a sophisticated audible conversation with the music. Ideally action can be timed by listening even with eyes closed.
4. Clear **anticipation → cue → exact hit impact → recovery / comedic miss** on the same musical timeline.
5. Strong framing, staged camera, readable silhouettes, distinctive palette/texture per game; scene occupies nearly the whole playable viewport.
6. Music that contains a memorable tune/hook and musical sections, not the same sine bass and hi-click repeating for 40 seconds.
7. Low on-screen text during active gameplay; put instructions and statistics in their own moments. No falling-note highway as the primary UI.
8. Each microgame's soundtrack, acting and image are deliberately synchronized. Difficulty variation changes song/cue patterns and acting, not just particle density.

Do not build a literal 1:1 remake of Karate Man, Fillbots, Ringside, Working Dough, or the 2026 Kitchen stage.

## 3. Global art direction — “hand-drawn comedy music show”

- **Drawing:** appealing flat 2D graphic illustration; thick purposeful contours (typically 3–6 logical px at reference canvas); bold shape language, expressive facial rig/sprites; restrained shadows and 1–2 texture accents. Do not use plain Godot primitives as the visible final art for every character. 2D skeletal rigs, keyframed sprites, vector-origin imported artwork or carefully designed painted procedural layers are acceptable.
- **Scene:** illustrated foreground and background props; at least 3 distinguishable depth layers where appropriate; dynamic actions independent of UI; musical beat-driven idle motion; no reliance on indiscriminate bloom or particle spam. 60 FPS on typical desktop, reasonable Web/mobile target.
- **Palette:** each game unique (industrial apricot/ink/sage; moonlit indigo/mint/coral; kitchen butter/yellow/cream/tomato/teal). Shared visual DNA can include warm off-white, graphite ink, and rhythmic star/spark motifs, but avoid one universal lavender button interface.
- **Typography:** intentionally chosen bundled **redistributable** display/body fonts with verified license and glyph coverage; clear hierarchy; no Unicode tofu. No huge constant text over the playfield.
- **Brand/menu:** a bespoke cartoon logo + one inviting animated mascot, strong visual hierarchy, playful timing animations and lightweight audio. Game-select uses **three illustrated stage poster cards** with scene art, title, one-line quirky hook and best score secondary. A selection should feel like picking a cartoon episode, not submitting a form. Settings/offline guide are subordinate options.
- **Playing:** full-bleed scene (target 75–90% of useful view); minimal discrete pause/settings overlays. Hide numeric score/combo/beat in normal play; F3 can reveal diagnostic UI, but not for ordinary players. Do not reserve giant 54–70 px button bars for PAUSE/RESTART/EXIT while playing; permit Space / click/tap anywhere for one-button games, keyboard A/L or left/right screen halves for Kitchen. A tiny accessible overlay or corner pause is enough. Use a proper pause modal (restart/exit) and support touch safe areas.
- **Onboarding:** brief illustrated 1–2 instruction cards + interactive 4–8 beat rehearsals, with audio-led examples (not just text). Retry rehearsal without changing core timing. Start quickly and let users learn by listening.
- **Results:** 3 unique animated vignettes and expressive grades, short original completion sting, best/perfect/clear statistics secondary. Animated, fast retry and return affordances. Do not clone Nintendo result art/text verbatim.
- **Responsive:** 960x640 and desktop 1440, plus 390x844 portrait. Stage composition must reframe instead of stretch/squash or clip key characters; stage can crop decorative background only. Tap targets accessible; reduced-motion respects cues.

## 4. Beat Factory — full stage storyboard

Existing mechanics: one input on moving packages; chart baseline **104 BPM / 42s / 69 events**. Maintain timing unless chart+music+QA are coherently updated.

**Original visual story:** a charming small factory run by a stubborn, expressive stamp-worker character (not Nintendo's Fillbots). A conveyor brings hand-drawn parcels bearing quirky shapes. Stamping rig has eyes/eyebrows and a giant squishy spring; a little supervisor bird watches from a window. Scene has real floor, wall, suspended light, conveyor teeth, moving workers, stamps on completed crates; palette apricot, cocoa, pale jade, paper cream.

**Action:** idle bobs on backbeat; parcels travel on beat; pre-hit stamp compresses subtly; hit = exaggerated downstroke + sharp ink-star burst + conveyor recoil + embossed mark + supervisor delight; Good = crooked ink stamp with wobble; Miss = squashed parcel or stamp air-punch + comic reaction. Recover smoothly and keep scene motion continuous. Introduce distinct musical variations at phrase boundaries (short duet with bird, conveyor speed gag, occasional anticipation), but preserve fairness.

**Music:** an **original** bouncy factory funk/retro-pop tune at 104 BPM: musical bass riff, drum kit kick/snare/closed hi-hat, plucked electric-piano-ish chords, brass-like lead/hook, subtle clockwork percussion. Musical 4- and 8-bar forms, instrumental fills and variation; stamp hit occupies a purposeful gap in arrangement, not randomly layered. Distinct crisp impact SFX and slightly different Good/Miss responses.

## 5. Echo Planet — full stage storyboard

Existing mechanics: auditory call for 4 beats, answer for 4 beats, six progressive rounds; baseline **96 BPM / 36s / 22 inputs / 22 call cues**. Study current chart call/response semantics before touching playback.

**Original visual story:** a tiny floating “planet choir” on an oversized blue night-sky stage: original smiling alien singer(s), blinking star audience, a moon instrument, layered comet trails, background color/nebula changes by musical phrase. Characters need multiple expressions and actual singing mouth shapes; do not ship the single purple face-disk.

**Action:** singer audibly sings a memorable short “ba/doo/la”-like **original** pitched call; stars light up syllable by syllable during LISTEN, then look expectantly at the player during RESPONSE; successful echo makes a constellation connect and stars dance; Good partial shimmer; Miss creates a friendly off-key / confused-antenna gag. Include obvious distinction between listening and response through voices, body language and lighting, not a text paragraph or moving note bar. Be especially careful that input cue language is synchronized with the actual chart.

**Music:** original dream-pop / music-box space groove at 96 BPM: warm kick, syncopated soft percussion, pulsing rounded bass, bell/mallet arpeggio, airy pad, memorable 4-bar melodic response. Layered distinct *call timbres* and associated pitch-coded SFX; leave space in the arrangement for the user response. No ripped or imitated signature Nintendo samples.

## 6. Rhythm Kitchen — full stage storyboard

Existing mechanics: left/right two-lane timing, baseline **102 BPM / 42s / 67 events**.

**Original visual story:** one lovable chef protagonist with oversized hat, a confident but goofy assistant, two **visually distinct** cooking stations (e.g. tomato-red frying pan, mint-blue mixing bowl), hand-drawn ingredients with expressions, tiled kitchen background, window, steam and hanging utensils. Unlike a DDR chart, vegetables should arrive as part of a believable comic kitchen performance.

**Action:** left/right use different chef hands/tools, character glance and posture; incoming ingredients follow distinct curved paths and cue sounds; precise catch = chef tosses ingredient in and the pot briefly celebrates (steam shape, aromatic star); wrong side = splash/flop and comic grimace; the final sequence shows a completed funny dish. Every ingredient's visual motion lands on event time; do not accidentally obscure target timing. No constantly visible huge left/right UI rectangles; keyboard A/L and left/right touch zones retained with small accessible mode hint.

**Music:** original jaunty kitchen bossa/ska/pop at 102 BPM: tight hand percussion, snappy snare/claps, clean rhythmic guitar/piano stabs, moving bass, short playful lead hook, appropriately pitched left/right "clack"/"plink" cooking sounds. Each correct action becomes part of the percussion groove; wrong inputs produce a short comic dissonance without stopping music.

## 7. Music production & synchronization standards

The existing `core/synth.gd` generates 11,025-Hz mono PCM using 75–92-Hz sine bass + identical 630-Hz click + quiet pad and 0.14-second beep grades. **This is a timing test signal, not a production soundtrack. Replace or expand it.**

- Deliver **three distinct, genuinely musical, original arrangements** of ~36–42s each with introduction, motif, rhythmic complexity progression, fill/bridge and finale. Do not merely raise sample rate of the old sine loop.
- Produce at **44.1kHz or 48kHz** (stereo preferred for background song; downmix-safe cues). Original layered composition with at least drums/percussion, bass, chordal harmony and lead hook, plus short distinct ear-consistent cue/SFX palette. Render locally and store in repo under `assets/audio/` with provenance manifest and reproducible source (MIDI/score/script/stems where feasible).
- Implement sample-accurate scheduling relative to one **authoritative audio clock**; maintain or improve Godot `RhythmSession` synchronization, input latency calibration, paused audio and Web audio start restrictions. Never shift music vs chart without re-evaluating all event timestamps. No visual-frame-driven judgement.
- At least 3 distinct outcome sounds per game (Perfect / Good / Miss), plus distinct contextual cue(s), intro and end sting; no generic identical beeps across 3 worlds. Mix should leave space for those sounds. Keep SFX onset tight with immediate action feedback.
- Prevent distortion, clipping, harsh continuously piercing treble, phase cancellation and abrupt clicks at sample transitions; use amplitude envelopes, soft limiting, controlled loudness and deliberate fades. Audible on normal PC speakers and headphones. Test Chrome WebAudio and Windows.
- Do not download or transcribe Nintendo recordings. Do not assume algorithmically generated music is “polished” without listening.

## 9. Quality gates and evidence — blocking

**Art acceptance**
- Actual game screenshots at 960x640 and 390x844 for home, select, each 3 gameplay scenes, and results. Screenshots should reveal a recognizable **illustrated scene** and expressive character actions without needing accompanying explanation. Stage + environment dominate viewport; utilities no longer dominate. No text/shape overlap or default-ui-looking stage.
- Each game has authored protagonist + environment + at least **idle, anticipation, Perfect, Good, Miss, recovery** visibly distinct acting states. Animated background/secondary prop and recognizable silhouettes.
- A real 15–30s captured gameplay clip per game at playable speed, showing repeated beat-synchronous changes, success/miss effects, and background motion. “Animated” must mean the scene visibly evolves, not just circles pulsing.

**Audio acceptance**
- Three 20s audible game clips or offline playable exports. Reviewer can distinguish three *songs* with memorable melodic motif, drum groove, harmony and phrase transitions. Per-game SFX matches character and is not the same generic tone.
- Full cue and judgement still work without looking at the screen for ordinary sections; call/response can be explained by ear.
- Audio onset timing and chart remain aligned; no audible pops on pause/resume/restart, no Web audio deadlock, reasonable first-load time and file size.

**Interaction acceptance**
- No constant giant Pause/Restart/Exit controls occupying a third of screen.
- Interactive practice and straightforward start/exit/retry; keyboard + touch both usable.
- Results are celebratory *scenes*, not just numbers.
- Regressions pass; no unsupported license/asset/provenance, no remote services. Offline PWA cold reload and Windows export still work.

**Review/handoff**
- Write `production/qa/ART_AUDIO_REVIEW.md` summarizing before/after evidence, audio filenames, screenshot paths, measured timings, acceptance result per game, exact commit SHA, tests actually run, known issues.
- Upload reviewable media only through agreed channels; avoid bloating Git history with huge uncompressed raw video or copyrighted references.
- Final status: VERIFIED only after code + technical tests + real visual & listening QA are evidenced; otherwise REVIEW_REQUIRED or BLOCKED with exact remaining work.
